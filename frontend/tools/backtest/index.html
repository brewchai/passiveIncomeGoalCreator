What this can and cannot tell you.

This is what happened once. It is not a prediction. India's share market record only goes back to 1999, so there are 26 years to test, and that is not enough to promise a safe withdrawal rate. What it shows well is the thing projections hide: the order the returns came in, and how much the first few years matter.

It leaves a lot out. No health shock, no wedding, no college fees, no house. No cutting back in a bad year, which would help. No panic selling in a crash, which would hurt more than anything here. Recent start years have had less time to go wrong, so they look better than they are.

The ₹10 crore version, with the reasoning behind each rule, is in the backtest article. To throw a crash at a plan that starts today, use the sequence risk calculator.

Where the numbers come from.

  • Market returns. Nifty 50 Total Return Index, calendar-year returns with dividends reinvested, from NSE Indices, as published in the Nifty 50 whitepaper. 2026 is the return to 2 November, a partial year.
  • Inflation. India's annual CPI from the World Bank series through 2025, and the 2026 figure from MoSPI's monthly releases, January to September.
  • Deposit rates. Term deposits of 1 to 3 years at major banks, as at the end of March each year, from the RBI Handbook of Statistics on the Indian Economy. Each year uses the midpoint of the published range.
  • Tax. 30% on deposit interest throughout. Equity gains at 10% to 2004, nil from 2005 to 2018, 10% above ₹1 lakh from 2019 with the 2018 grandfathering, and 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh from 2025, following the rules as laid out by ClearTax.

Every series sits in one file on this page, with its source next to it. If a figure disagrees with the published one, tell me and I will fix it.
