What this can and cannot tell you.

This is one history, not a probability. India's total return record only starts in 1999, so 26 years is all there is, and 26 years cannot hand you a safe withdrawal rate with the confidence the long American studies claim. What it can do is show you the thing every smooth projection hides: the order the returns arrived in, and how the first few years decide the rest.

It does not model a health shock, a wedding, a child's college, a house, or your own inflation running hotter than the official number. It does not model the two things real people do, which cut both ways: trimming spending in a bad year, which helps, and selling everything in the middle of a crash, which is worse than anything in this table. Later start years have had fewer years of spending, so their bars flatter them.

The full write-up of the ₹10 crore version, with the reasoning behind each rule, is in the backtest article. To throw a crash at a plan from today rather than the past, use the sequence risk calculator.

Where the numbers come from.

  • Market returns. Nifty 50 Total Return Index, calendar-year returns with dividends reinvested, from NSE Indices, as published in the Nifty 50 whitepaper. 2026 is the return to 2 November, a partial year.
  • Inflation. India's annual CPI from the World Bank series through 2025, and the 2026 figure from MoSPI's monthly releases, January to September.
  • Deposit rates. Term deposits of 1 to 3 years at major banks, as at the end of March each year, from the RBI Handbook of Statistics on the Indian Economy. Each year uses the midpoint of the published range.
  • Tax. 30% on deposit interest throughout. Equity gains at 10% to 2004, nil from 2005 to 2018, 10% above ₹1 lakh from 2019 with the 2018 grandfathering, and 12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh from 2025, following the rules as laid out by ClearTax.
